Market size

How big is quick commerce in India's grocery market?

Quick commerce is only about 1% of India's grocery market. The other 99% still goes through shops and markets.

  • Quick-commerce orders were worth about ₹64,000 crore in FY25.
  • CareEdge forecasts about ₹2 lakh crore by FY28, roughly 3× in three years.
  • For kirana shops, the window to start home delivery and WhatsApp orders is still open, but closing.

Source: CareEdge report, July 2025, via Outlook Business.

How it works

What is a dark store?

A dark store is a small warehouse that only fulfils app orders. Customers can't walk in. Apps place dark stores within a couple of kilometres of homes so orders can be packed and delivered in minutes.

  • India has more than 5,500 dark stores (Bernstein). CareEdge counted 3,072 in FY25; counts differ by source.
  • Each store pays rent, staff and riders whether orders come or not, so it needs enough orders nearby to make money.
  • The real game is order density, not speed.

Sources: Bernstein report; CareEdge, July 2025.

Your bill

What fees do Zepto, Blinkit and Instamart charge?

On top of item prices, quick-commerce apps have charged delivery fees, handling fees, surge fees, rain fees and small-cart fees. Fees are now a competitive weapon.

  • In November 2025, Zepto removed its handling, surge and rain fees.
  • Zepto also cut its free-delivery minimum from ₹199 to ₹99.
  • Tip for shoppers: check the full bill before you pay. Tip for shops: state delivery charges upfront, with no surprise fees.

Source: Zepto announcement and news coverage, November 2025.

Startup story

How did Grofers become Blinkit?

Grofers changed its name and its business model, and won. It started in 2013, switched to 10-minute delivery and renamed itself Blinkit in 2021.

  • Zomato acquired Blinkit in 2022 in a deal worth about $568 million.
  • In FY25, Blinkit reported ₹5,206 crore in revenue across 153 cities.
  • Lesson: if the old model isn't working, don't be afraid to change it.

Source: Zomato / Eternal filings.

Startup lesson

Why did Dunzo shut down?

Dunzo had big investors but lost money on its orders. Funding is not a business model; every order needs to make a profit.

  • Founded in Bengaluru in 2014; Google invested in 2017.
  • Reliance invested $200 million for a 25.8% stake in 2022.
  • FY23: revenue ₹227 crore, loss ₹1,802 crore. After layoffs and a co-founder exit, the app went offline in 2025.

Source: company filings and news coverage.

Startup lesson

Why did Getir fail?

Getir grew fast but never made money per order. Small baskets, free delivery and big discounts brought growth without profit, while every dark store kept paying rent, staff and riders.

  • Valued at $11.8 billion in 2022 and operating in 9 countries, including London, Berlin and New York.
  • In 2024 it exited every country except Turkey.
  • A 2026 Uber deal valued it at up to $435 million, less than 4% of its peak value.

Source: news coverage of Getir's funding, exits and the Uber deal.

Founder story

How did Zepto start?

Zepto's founders, Aadit Palicha and Kaivalya Vohra, started at 17 with a business called KiranaKart, then left Stanford to build Zepto full time.

  • Zepto's valuation went from about $2.5 million in 2021 to about $7 billion in October 2025.
  • Update: in July 2026 Zepto reportedly raised money at about $4.5 billion, with its IPO on hold.
  • Lesson: age isn't the key; fast execution is.

Sources: funding news; MediaNama, December 2025; YourStory (PTI report), July 2026.

Local shops

Are kirana shops closing because of quick commerce?

A distributors' federation claims they are. The All India Consumer Products Distributors Federation (AICPDF) claimed in October 2024 that about 2 lakh kirana shops had closed, and alleged predatory pricing by apps. This is their claim, not an independent count.

  • By their estimate: 45% of closures in metros, 30% in tier-1 cities, 25% in tier-2 and tier-3 towns.
  • Local shops still have something apps don't: customers know and trust them.
  • Practical steps: start home delivery, take WhatsApp orders and look after regular customers.

Source: AICPDF statement, October 2024 (presented as their claim).

Small towns

Are Flipkart and Amazon bringing quick commerce to small towns?

Yes. Flipkart and Amazon have both entered quick commerce, and the biggest growth is coming from smaller markets.

  • Flipkart Minutes: about 1,000 dark stores in 130+ cities, reached in under two years.
  • Amazon Now: in 15+ cities, with plans for about 100.
  • For local shops in towns like Manjeri: get ready now with home delivery, online orders and fast replies.

Source: company announcements.

Shopping habits

What did Swiggy Instamart's 2025 report show?

Big orders make the news, but everyday items like milk and curry leaves drive the business.

  • Biggest single order: ₹4.3 lakh for three iPhone 17s, in Hyderabad.
  • Smallest order: a ₹10 printout, in Bengaluru.
  • Milk was the most-ordered item, at more than 4 packets every second.
  • A user in Kochi ordered curry leaves 368 times in the year (from Swiggy's own report).

Sources: Swiggy Instamart, How India Instamarted 2025; Storyboard18, December 2025.
